_"Donor-cycle"_

_"Not if, but when"_

_"My uncle's cousin's brother died on one of those"_

These are all things people have told me. One friend's mom told my friend to run over my bike so I wouldn't ride anymore.

## What riding gives me

When everyone I know is busy, I find myself hopping on my bike for a late-night ride. I cruise through wealthy neighborhoods, looking at the expensive homes and wondering what my future will hold. Then I explore Houston, finding scenic spots to admire. On the way home, I open it up — the quick twist of the throttle, the engine firing just inches beneath me, the shift through each gear — until I feel as one with the machine.

My rides usually end right back where I started, but with a little more perspective.

## The dangers

I recognize that riding isn't for everyone. It carries a real risk that driving a car doesn't have — and that's ok. Every time I throw my leg over the saddle, I come to a silent understanding that I may not walk away from the ride. People think that the dangers of riding are outside my control, but some of them aren't. Here is what I do so they worry less:

1. Wear my helmet
2. Wear other protective gear
3. Be a fair-weather rider
4. Ride the speed limit
5. Ride sober

None of it guarantees I'll make it home from every ride, but it's everything I can do.

## Why I ride

I know my friends and family express concern because they love me. They don't want to see me get hurt or worse. Riding is a risk, but without it my life would feel empty. I wouldn't have the time to cruise slowly and think of the future, the chance to look up and see what's around me, or the feeling of being alive on the ride home.

I don't just live to ride.

I ride to live.
